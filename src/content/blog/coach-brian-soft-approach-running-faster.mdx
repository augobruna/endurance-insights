Every so often, you have a conversation that just clicks. One that gives you a new lens through which to see your sport. Our recent chat with running coach Brian Boisvert for Human Endurance was one of those moments.

[Listen to Human Endurance](https://tr.ee/uyUohZ7SKY)

We went into it expecting to talk about marathon training, pacing, and the usual coaching topics. And we did. But the thread that wove through the entire conversation was a philosophy that feels both revolutionary and incredibly simple: the **“soft approach to hard goals”.**

In a culture that constantly screams “no pain, no gain,” Brian’s perspective felt like a breath of fresh air. He talked about moving beyond the obsession with finish times and learning to celebrate the goals in the in between - like finding structure in your life or proving to yourself that you can stick to a plan.

His analogy for managing a race or a tough workout has been stuck in my head ever since:

> “When you’re running too hot at mile 16 of a marathon, the smartest thing you can do isn’t to double down and grind yourself into dust. It’s to have the grace to back off slightly, collect yourself, and then attack again when you’ve recovered” - Brian Boisvert

It’s not about being weak; it’s about being smart. It’s about playing the long game.

We also touched on the deeper, transformative power of running. Brian shared his own story of how the sport helped him build confidence, and how he now works to create inclusive, safe spaces for queer runners and others to find their place in the community. It was a powerful reminder that running is so much more than just the miles we log.

This was a conversation about building a life where running adds energy and joy, rather than just being another source of pressure. I truly think the insights he shared can help any athlete, at any level, build a healthier and more successful relationship with the sport.

As we do at the end of every episode, here are our key takeaways for listeners:

## **Key Takeaways for Listeners:**

- **Master the Basics First:** Before you worry about the latest recovery tech or nutrition hack, ask yourself: are you being consistent? The biggest performance gains will always come from mastering the fundamentals of showing up and listening to your body.
- **A “Soft Approach” is a Smart Approach:** When training gets hard, being “soft” means having the wisdom to adjust, focus on effort instead of pace, and live to train effectively another day. This is how you avoid burnout and injury.
- **Redefine Your Success:** Your finish time is just one data point. Celebrate the discipline you’re building, the consistency you’re showing, and the way training structures your life for the better. These are the wins that keep you in the sport for years to come.
- **Find Your Community:** Running can be a solo pursuit, but its power is magnified when shared. Finding a community where you feel you belong isn’t just a bonus - it’s a fundamental part of staying motivated and enjoying the journey.
